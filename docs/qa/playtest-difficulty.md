# Playtesting Pocket Planet with kids at home

This is a guide for the owner (a parent, not a researcher) to run short, friendly playtests with children ages 6-10. No special skills needed, no recording, no personal data. Just watching a kid play for a few minutes and jotting down what you see.

## 1. Why we do this, and what we measure

Pocket Planet is built for kids 6+. We want each planet to feel like a good challenge: not so easy a child gets bored, not so hard they get frustrated and want to quit. Our game bots (little simulated players) already check the numbers, but bots aren't children — a bot can't tell us if a kid understood what just happened, or whether they enjoyed it.

So a handful of times during development, we sit a real child down for about 15 minutes and watch. We're checking two simple things:

- **Did they get it?** Can they say, in their own words, what a Trouble or an obstacle did, and what stops it?
- **Did it feel right?** After certain planets, we ask them to point at a face that shows how that planet felt — too easy, too hard, or just right.

That's it. No quiz, no timer pressure, no wrong answers. We're watching the game, not testing the child.

## 2. Before you start

A few ground rules to keep this simple, kind, and private:

- **Get the parent's OK first.** If the child playing isn't your own, ask their parent or guardian, and explain it's just watching them play a game for about 15 minutes.
- **15-20 minutes, max.** Shorter is fine. Stop earlier if the child is tired, bored, or asks to stop.
- **The child can stop any time, no reason needed.** This isn't a test they can fail — if they want to go play outside instead, that's a totally fine result.
- **No recording.** No photos or videos of faces or voices. Paper notes only.
- **No names.** Write "Child A", "Child B", "Child C", etc. Never a real name, age-plus-name, or anything that could identify who played.
- **Sound is optional.** Let the child choose whether sound is on or off — either is fine to test.
- **Reduce Motion is the child's preference.** If they already have it on (or find flashy animation overwhelming), leave it as they like it.
- **Prepping the device:** you don't need to touch any settings or files yourself. Just message Claude first and say something like _"ask Claude for a test save at planet N"_ (swap N for whichever planet you want the session to start near — see the planet lists in section 3). Claude will set up a fresh install or a test save at the right spot so you're not hunting through menus with a kid waiting.

## 3. The face card

After certain planets, pause and show the child five simple faces in a row, and ask them to point at the one that matches how that planet felt. Print this row out, or just draw it on paper — no need for the real device screen:

```
 😴          🙂          😀          😬          😣
Too easy   A bit easy   Just right   A bit hard   Too hard
```

Describe them in words if it helps: the sleepy face means "that was boring, way too easy"; the open smile in the middle means "that felt just right"; the scrunched face at the end means "that was really too hard."

**What to say.** Keep it neutral and simple — don't lead them toward an answer:

> "How did that one feel?"

That's the whole question. Don't say "that was hard, right?" or "wasn't that fun?" — just point at the row of faces and let them pick.

**When to show it.** Right after the child finishes one of these planets (win or lose):

| Milestone being tested       | Show the face card after planets |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| M7 (Fusions and Combos)      | 8, 22                            |
| M7.5 (sky obstacles)         | 8, 22, 33, 41                    |
| M8 (Troubles and difficulty) | 14, 19, 26, 36                   |

If you're running one session that covers a few of these milestones, just show the card after every planet in the combined list: **8, 14, 19, 22, 26, 33, 36, 41.**

## 4. What to watch and note, without helping

While the child plays, watch quietly. Don't coach, don't point at the screen, don't say "try this instead." If they're stuck, let them be stuck for a bit — that's useful information too. Jot down what you notice:

- **Taps that miss.** Did they try to tap something and miss it? How many times?
- **Confusion.** Did they pause, frown, ask "what do I do?", or look at you for help?
- **Did they read the landing card / red badge?** Before throwing, did they seem to look at the warning card or badge, or just fling right away?
- **Did they wait for a gap?** On planets with moving obstacles, did they pause and time their throw, or throw regardless of what was moving?
- **Did they use a creature on purpose?** Did they seem to pick a throw _because_ a particular creature could handle a Trouble (e.g. "the fire one goes here"), or was it random?
- **Can they say what it does, in their own words?** After the planet, ask simply: _"What did the [rock / vent / whatever they just saw] do?"_ Write down roughly what they say — you're checking whether they have any working idea, not grading their vocabulary.
- **Fails and retries.** How many times did they fail the planet, and did they try again on their own or want to stop?
- **When they wanted to stop.** Note the planet number and roughly why (bored, frustrated, tired, or just naturally done).

## 6. How to read the results, and what to send back

Once you've run all five children (or as many as you managed), here's how the numbers turn into a pass or a flag. You don't need to do any math beyond finding a middle value.

**"Median" just means the middle answer.** Line up the five children's face-card picks for one planet from easiest to hardest (Too easy → A bit easy → Just right → A bit hard → Too hard) and take the one in the middle (the 3rd of 5). That's the median for that planet.

**"An end" means Too easy or Too hard** — the two outer faces on the row.

The targets we're checking against, planet by planet:

- **Planets 8 and 22** (Fusions/Combos, M7): median should land on "Just right", and no more than 1 of the 5 children should pick an end (Too easy or Too hard).
- **Planets 8, 22, 33 and 41** (sky obstacles, M7.5): same face-card target as above, **plus** at least 4 of the 5 children should be able to say what each obstacle they met does, in their own words (Table 2).
- **Planets 14, 19, 26 and 36** (Troubles and the difficulty program, M8): median should be "Just right" and at most 1 of 5 an end, **plus** at least 4 of 5 children can say what a Trouble did and what stopped it, **plus** at least 3 of 5 use a creature on purpose by the third Trouble planet they meet.

If a planet's median lands on an end face, or 2 or more children pick an end, or fewer than 4 of 5 can say what something does — that's not a failure of the child, it's a flag for us to look at the planet's design. Just note it plainly; we'll take it from there.

**What to send back.** No personal data needed — just the results:

- A photo of your filled-in paper tally sheet, **or**
- The filled tables above, pasted into a message to Claude (copy the tables with your answers filled in).

Either way, please make sure no child's real name appears anywhere — Child A/B/C/D/E only. Thank you for running this — it's the only way we get to see the game through a kid's eyes instead of a bot's.
